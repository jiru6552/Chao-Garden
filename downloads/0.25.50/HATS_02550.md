# Vanilla Chao hats — 0.25.50

## Items and appearance

Use `minecraft:carved_pumpkin`, `minecraft:melon` (the whole block), and `minecraft:skeleton_skull` as Chao headwear. These items use the existing offer, consideration, wardrobe-consent and wearing phases. They also appear in the Analyzer's existing wardrobe inventory selector. Shears or wardrobe removal return the actual vanilla stack, with its components, rather than generating a separate cosmetic item.

The 0.25.49 checkout contains one separate item in this group: `chao_garden:watermelon_hat`. It contains no registered pumpkin/skull hat items or standalone pumpkin/skull hat models; the Born in Chaos variant's pumpkin-themed crest is unrelated and is unchanged. No fictitious old pumpkin/skull IDs were introduced.

Vanilla melon retains the previous SA2 watermelon hat's measured source mesh and fitting, so replacing the inventory item does not replace its established worn appearance. Carved pumpkin uses Minecraft's carved block model and textures, facing forward. Skeleton skull uses Minecraft's actual skeleton skull model and texture. Both new presentations follow the animated skull joint and fit the current head envelope with 0.3 model pixels of padding. Their closed shells temporarily conceal animal ears, crown/forehead, horns and face panels in both legacy and CWE overlay layers, preventing protrusions through the mask. Removing the mask shows the same saved animal parts again; no DNA or appearance data is erased. The melon and other existing hats retain their previous presentation. Existing source-head, morph, age and themed-variant fitting remain in the renderer. The layer respects invisibility, cocoons and source-comparison mode.

The uncarved pumpkin, jack o'lantern, melon slices, glistering melon slices and other mob/player heads are unchanged. In particular, `minecraft:melon_slice` retains its normal food component and does not become a wardrobe item. Other mod foods are not classified by their names.

## Older saves

The old `watermelon_hat` registry ID remains as a hidden compatibility item to decode old inventories safely. It has no market listing or creative entry. Its inventory presentation now points to Minecraft's melon item model. The legacy ID is still addressable by administrative commands, as required for save decoding; ordinary gameplay acquires and uses vanilla melon.

Conversion changes the top-level item ID to `minecraft:melon`, preserving the exact count and complete explicit data-component patch. Names, lore, custom data and nested container components are retained. One compatibility exception preserves the old unstackable default if an old hat has a foreign/custom damage component: a normal melon's default stack size of 64 would make that otherwise valid damageable item fail Minecraft's component validation. Normal hats become normally stackable vanilla melons. The conversion does not recursively rewrite nested inventories or arbitrary foreign NBT.

Conversion occurs when:

- A Chao loads old worn or held equipment from entity NBT, including carrier/companion snapshots. Joining the level also normalizes equipment.
- A legacy stack ticks in a player's inventory, including armor/offhand slots, or in an equipped living-entity slot.
- A dropped legacy item entity joins the server level, including an old saved drop.
- A player directly offers a legacy stack before its next inventory tick, or a Chao begins holding that legacy gift.
- A worn legacy stack is returned through the shared wardrobe transaction.

Unopened or unloaded containers and nested item components are left untouched. An old token inside such storage remains under the compatibility ID until extracted and handled by one of these paths. The normal extraction-to-player/drop/use paths then convert it. No global chunk or inventory sweep is performed. Other mods that keep items in private inventories indefinitely may retain the hidden compatibility item until they expose it to a supported path. Existing user worlds were not bulk converted, and downgrading a converted save is not guaranteed.

An old in-progress wardrobe gift retains its giver and accepted decision when it loads; it completes with a real vanilla melon. Swapping and removal detach the authoritative slot before inventory/world callbacks. If a full inventory and a cancelled drop prevent return, the exact headgear is restored for a later retry, rather than lost or duplicated.

Saved Black Market offers are validated against the current stock version and catalog; old watermelon-hat offers are replaced by a normal restock. Other hat prices are preserved. No pumpkin, melon or skeleton hat is sold by the Black Market.

## Verification scope

`HatGameTests050` supplies 19 native server checks: 12 lifecycle combinations across three hats, baby/adult and both interaction hands, plus seven migration, component preservation, consent, ownership, cancelled-return and unrelated-item checks. It exercises actual entity/component codecs, carrier snapshots, the real item-gift transaction, inventory ownership and world item publication. The fixtures accelerate the existing item phase clock, without implementing separate equip behavior.

`HatVisualSmoke050` supplies 40 actual-client cases: 36 combinations of the three canonical hats across 12 baby/adult, alignment, Chaos, character and animal-head forms, plus four focused parrot-beak and boar-face cases under each new closed mask. It uses multiple camera views and an entity save/load before rendering. Its render callback checks the actual legacy/CWE overlay pose visibility, retention of non-head limbs, unchanged melon behavior and the complete saved Chao state. The main 0.25.50 report records actual execution results and observations. Test availability or geometry assertions alone are not a visual acceptance claim.

Still inspect your preferred variants, resource pack, movement transitions and remote-player view. Old nested storage can be checked in a copy of your own save. Independent multiplayer and private inventories from every third-party mod are outside the native fixtures' coverage.
