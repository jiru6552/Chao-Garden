# Lobby gate and transporter refinements

The oblique transporter screenshot exposed separate rendering passes for glass tube walls, spiral discs and energy ribbons. The lower disc had no depth write, and the tube walls were flushed before later effects. The renderer now submits both physical disc surfaces with depth writes, completes opaque architecture first, and submits crown glass, tube walls and ribbons together in one native sorted translucent batch. Each tube wall is divided into fifteen short strips so center-distance sorting can follow the nearby ribbon segments at steep angles; there are no extra internal glass caps. Both discs retain their two-sided mesh and their existing spiral material. The glass lobby floor remains translucent.

The native diagnostic also exposed a separate global ordering defect: a distant Kindergarten End-portal surface erased the foreground tube only inside its aperture. The renderer now captures each visible scene anchor's camera-relative transform and submits its shared glass/ribbon batch in the client `AFTER_PARTICLES` stage, after End-portal surfaces and opaque geometry have established depth. The queue is cleared at the start and end of each frame. The tube material still blends without writing depth, so the distant star texture remains visible through it rather than being discarded.

The garden gate has a connected stone plinth from the lobby floor to its pale statue platforms. The earlier round platforms began 0.46 blocks above the floor. The urns now rest directly on the same plinth. The floating emotion orb above each sculpture remains part of the Chao design.

`LobbyGeometry0252` supplies the geometry consumed by the actual renderer: a closed three-dimensional teardrop head with one apex, an urn shell with an annular lip and visible inner bowl, leaves that rise through the open pot before curving outward, and five continuous stalk paths from soil to petalled blossoms. The old head was an extruded triangle, which produced a blunt ridge from side angles. The former leaves began at the rim and drooped through the pot wall, and the flower spheres had no stalks.

The pure geometry suite checks closed surfaces, a unique head apex, contact between the feet and supported platform, the open bowl, and densely sampled leaf/stalk clearance through the upper pot. The renderer contract checks that the native scene consumes that geometry, writes the spiral-disc depths and keeps the glass/effects together in a sorted batch. These static checks are separate from native visual signoff; oblique transporter sweeps, low statue-support views, and close urn/head views are required in the current release study.
